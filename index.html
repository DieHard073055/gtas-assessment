01 · Introduction and technical background

What GTAS is, and why it exists.

What it is

A pre-arrival passenger risk assessment system

Receives airline API and PNR data, screens every traveller against risk rules and watchlists, and gives analysts a ranked list of passengers to examine before the flight lands.

RA
Why it exists

UN Security Council Resolutions 2178 & 2396

States must use Advance Passenger Information and build the capability to collect and use Passenger Name Record data to detect terrorist and criminal travel.

UN
Who provides it

Built by US CBP · supplied by the WCO

Donated to the WCO and supplied free to member administrations. Maldives Customs moved from manual selection to GTAS in June 2019, funded by Japan through the WCO.

$0
Sources: WCO News 90 (Oct 2019); MCS presentation, M. Afzal, Chief Customs Officer; WCO newsroom (Jul 2019).

03 · Critical evaluation: drawbacks and limitations

Strong core. Weak edges.

limits effectiveness nowconstrains growth

Technical

Rules do not learn

Static criteria go stale; false positives grow.

Batch, not real time

No interactive API. A suspect still boards in Doha.

Heavy stack, small team

Java, Drools, Neo4j, Elastic to patch and secure.

Name matching

Mohamed, Mohammed, Muhammad. Transliteration noise.

Data

EU-origin PNR blocked

Reported by MCS. No Maldives–EU agreement.

Air only

No vessel, crew or yacht data in an ocean state.

IMUGA unused

Traveller declarations are not cross-checked.

Carrier quality

Late onboarding, missing fields, grace periods.

Operational

No outcome loop

Search results never return to tune the rules.

Gateway coverage

Gan and Hanimaadhoo add new entry points.

Peak-bank overload

Hits arrive when the hall is busiest.

Few performance KPIs

No public hit-to-find measures.

Governance

No data protection law

Bill only submitted to the Majlis in May 2026.

Donor dependence

Upgrades rely on WCO and CBP goodwill.

Single data contractor

Three-year term from 2019. Renewal is a risk.

Bias oversight

No formal audit of rules for discrimination.

03 · Critical evaluation · three blind spots

What GTAS cannot see.

Blind spot 1

The sea

1,190 islands and a vast EEZ. GTAS reads aircraft only. Yachts, dhonis, fishing and cargo vessels never enter the risk picture.

Blind spot 2

Europe

MCS reported airlines cannot send PNR for EU-origin flights. Without a Maldives–EU arrangement, key routes are screened on identity only.

PNRGOV LHR → MLE ✕ transfer refused GDPR · no agreement PNRGOV FRA → MLE ✕ transfer refused GDPR · no agreement PAXLST FRA → MLE ✓ received
Blind spot 3

The future of the code

The public GTAS repository now returns 404. Upgrades, patches and roadmap depend on others.

04 · Opportunities · privacy-enhancing cryptography

Send the questions to the data. Not the data to the questions.

Sealed enclave in the EU

MCS rules run inside attested hardware. No one, including the host, sees the data.

Private set intersection

Watchlist checks where each side learns only the overlap.

Only hits leave

Every other passenger stays in Europe. Every disclosure is logged.

Limits to state plainly

Supports, not replaces, an EU agreement and the Data Protection Bill. CJEU C-817/19 (2022): pre-set criteria only, no self-learning models on EU PNR.

Stabilise. Integrate. Then make it intelligent.

Conclusion · the assessment in three lines

Three things to take away.

1

GTAS is technically sound and operationally proven.

Pre-arrival API/PNR screening, analyst rules, watchlists and link analysis. At MCS: an interception ten weeks after go-live.

2

Its limitations are real and specific.

Static rules, batch processing, air-only data, blocked EU PNR, no outcome feedback, and dependence on others for the code.

3

Keep GTAS. Extend it.

Stabilise, integrate, then add explainable intelligence, with privacy built in and a human always deciding.
